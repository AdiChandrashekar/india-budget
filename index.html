India · Public spending on education

The target, since 1966

6%

India's oldest education promise

In 1966 a national commission said India should spend six per cent of its national income on education, and every education policy since has repeated it. Across the two decades the government's own accounts cover, public spending has never passed of GDP — and most of it is spent by the governments the argument rarely mentions.

The account heads every government shares

Major head 2202 means general education in a state budget exactly as it does in the Union's. That shared classification is what makes it possible to compare the two governments at all, and it is the basis of chapter three.

What the figures cover

Reading them

  • Never add the Union's and the states' education budgets. Money the Union passes to the states appears in both. The only sound combined figure counts each rupee once, by the government that spends it — chapter one's.
  • Rupees at current prices. Rupee totals are not adjusted for inflation, so later years look larger than they are. Shares of GDP and of total spending are not affected.
  • Solid lines are audited spending, dashed lines are estimates. The most recent years are always estimates.

Where the numbers come from

  • The Union: the Expenditure Budget and Demands for Grants, on indiabudget.gov.in.
  • The states: the Reserve Bank of India's State Finances: A Study of Budgets.
  • Centre and states together: the Ministry of Education's Analysis of Budgeted Expenditure on Education, the only official series that counts each rupee once.
  • GDP: the RBI Handbook of Statistics on the Indian Economy.

Claims from outside these figures, and their sources

Everything else on this page is calculated from the sources above. These statements are not, so each is attributed.

  • The 6% of GDP target. Proposed by the Education Commission of 1964-66 (the Kothari Commission), adopted in the National Policy on Education 1968, repeated in 1986, and committed to again by the National Education Policy 2020, which aims to reach 6% of GDP “at the earliest”.
  • “2.7 per cent”. The Economic Survey 2024-25's figure for public spending on education, on the measure of education departments alone. It matches the narrower count in chapter one.
  • “About four per cent”. PRS Legislative Research, Demand for Grants 2026-27: Education, citing World Bank data for 2022: “Centre and states together spend around 4.1% of GDP on education, lower than the recommended 6%.” It matches the wider count.
  • The states' share of central taxes rose from 32% to 42% from 2015-16, on the Fourteenth Finance Commission's recommendation.
  • Union money for jointly run schemes passes through state budgets from 2014-15. From that year, central assistance for schemes the states implement has been budgeted together and transferred through the states' consolidated funds, under Ministry of Finance guidelines of 6 January 2014, as set out by the Delhi government's planning department.
  • Samagra Shiksha is cost-shared 60:40 between the centre and the states, 90:10 for the north-eastern and Himalayan states, per PRS as above.
