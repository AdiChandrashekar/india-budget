Education, three ways

There is no single education budget

Three defensible definitions, three different numbers, all for the same year and the same government. Which one a headline uses changes the answer by tens of thousands of crore, so each series here is labelled with the definition behind it.

Budgeted against spent

The budget estimate is not the spending

Each year is published three times: as a budget estimate in February, as a revised estimate a year later, and as an audited actual the year after that. They rarely agree, and the gap runs both ways — a revised estimate can cut a programme mid-year or top it up. Quoting the budget estimate as though it were spending is the most common error in writing about this money.

Where the money sits in the accounts

Most of the education ministry's money is not booked as education

This is the trap that makes Union and state education figures irreconcilable. The ministry's largest single account head is grants-in-aid to state governments, not general education: the money is handed to states, which then book the spending in their own budgets under education. Add the two governments' education totals together and you count the same rupee twice.

What this covers, and what it does not

  • Union only, for now. State budgets are the next layer; they need RBI's State Finances: A Study of Budgets, not this pipeline.
  • Nominal rupees. No inflation adjustment and no share of GDP yet, because neither a deflator nor a GDP series is loaded. Shares of total Union expenditure are computed and are safe to compare across years.
  • Never add Union and state education totals. Central transfers appear in both. For a combined figure, the sources are the ministry's Analysis of Budgeted Expenditure on Education or the CAG's combined accounts.
