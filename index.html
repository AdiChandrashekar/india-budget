The target, since 1966

6%

India's oldest education promise

Six per cent of GDP on education. Proposed in 1966, restated in 1968, 1986 and 2020, never reached. What the country actually spends, who spends it, and why nobody can say exactly how far short it falls.

Reading the budget

Chapter one

A target nobody can measure

6% of GDP. The Education Commission of 1964-66 set it as the level of public spending on education India should reach. Three national policies have repeated it since.
2.7% is what India spends by the Economic Survey's count for 2023-24 - the money that flows through education departments.
4.1% is the World Bank figure for 2022, as cited by PRS Legislative Research - centre and states together.
4.6% is the Ministry of Education's own broader measure for 2020-21, which adds education and training run by other ministries.
1.9 points separate the lowest reading from the highest. The gap between the measurements is larger than most annual changes anyone argues about.

Chapter five

A budget estimate is not spending

Every year is published three times: a budget estimate in February, a revised estimate a year later, an audited actual the year after that. The gap between the first and the last runs in both directions.

Chapter six

Most of the ministry's money becomes state money

The Ministry of Education's largest account head is not education. It is grants-in-aid to state governments, which the states then spend and book as education themselves.

Coverage

Read this before comparing

  • Never add Union and state education totals. Central transfers appear in both. For a combined figure the sources are the ministry's Analysis of Budgeted Expenditure on Education or the CAG's combined accounts.
  • Nominal rupees. No inflation adjustment and no share of GDP computed here yet. Shares of total expenditure are computed and are safe to compare across years.

Where the numbers come from

  • Union figures are read from indiabudget.gov.in, the Expenditure Budget and Demands for Grants, 24 editions.
  • State figures are read from the Reserve Bank of India's State Finances: A Study of Budgets e-STATES database and Statement 26.
  • The Union total reproduces to within Rs 0.19 crore across 102 demands, and all 570 percentages RBI publishes in Statement 26 reproduce exactly.

Claims from outside the data, and their sources

Every other figure on this page is computed from the budget documents above. These are not, so each is attributed. Where published figures disagree, all are given rather than one being picked.

  • The 6% of GDP target. Proposed by the Education Commission of 1964-66 (the Kothari Commission), adopted in the National Policy on Education 1968, repeated in 1986, and committed to again by the National Education Policy 2020, which states the aim of reaching 6% of GDP "at the earliest".
  • 2.7% of GDP. Economic Survey 2024-25, for 2023-24, on the narrower measure of what central and state governments spend through their education departments.
  • 4.1% of GDP. PRS Legislative Research, Demand for Grants 2026-27: Education, citing World Bank data for 2022: "Centre and states together spend around 4.1% of GDP on education, lower than the recommended 6%."
  • 4.6% of GDP. The Ministry of Education's Analysis of Budgeted Expenditure on Education measure for 2020-21, which adds education and training spending by departments other than education - health, tribal affairs, labour and others.
  • Devolution rose from 32% to 42%. The Fourteenth Finance Commission's recommendation, effective from 2015-16, raising the states' share of the divisible pool of central taxes.
  • Samagra Shiksha is cost-shared 60:40. 60:40 between centre and states, 90:10 for the north-eastern and Himalayan states, and 100% central funding for union territories without a legislature, per PRS as above. The sharing pattern for its predecessor Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan was reset at the same time as the devolution change.
  • Scheme shares of the ministry's budget. Samagra Shiksha Rs 42,100 crore and PM POSHAN Rs 12,750 crore in 2026-27, per PRS as above.
